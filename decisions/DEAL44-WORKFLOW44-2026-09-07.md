## 1 — What this is

A shared checklist for one property transaction. The broker opens a room, every party (buyer, seller, both lawyers, the mortgage advisor) gets their own private link, and each deadline reaches the person responsible before it passes. Hebrew and right-to-left first, because the first market is Israel; an English twin for everywhere else.

Priced at ₪2,500 per transaction in Phase 0, set up and run by Moses. ₪349/month per broker once the rooms run themselves.

The source is a founder plan, "DEAL44 → WORKFLOW44, the 12-month solo introvert path to six figures": Phase 0 sell ten manual rooms in 14 days (gate: ₪15,000 or pivot), Phase 1 the MVP, Phase 2 scale, Phase 3 abstract the real-estate template into one of many and ship a workflow builder.

**Two founder decisions shaped the build:** Phase 0 is delivered on the existing stack rather than Airtable and Softr, so nothing is rebuilt in week three; and the product is **not limited to Israel** — the core is market-neutral with jurisdiction packs, Israel is simply market #1 and bills in shekels.

## 2 — What already existed, and what that saved

The plan's Phase 1 was roughly 70% written before this session started. Verified against the live database rather than the file headers, which are stale:

- `deals`, `deal_documents`, `deal_facts`, `deal_findings` are **applied and empty** on `ydghhcuuopqzgqcicubg`, despite `20260823_deal_intelligence.sql` reading as unapplied. So is `trio_properties`. That head already carries jurisdiction, deal type, property and provenance-mandatory facts.
- The closing engine — business-day maths, 44 checklist tasks across four transaction types, the 90/60/30/7 alert tiers — existed **twice**, verbatim, in `apps/closeflow` and `apps/leaseparse`. DEAL44 would have been the third copy.
- `@bizlegal/deal-engine` already had the `JurisdictionPack` shape with its `reviewed: false` gate, and `apps/hub/lib/deal-audit` already demonstrated the refuse-while-unreviewed pattern.

What did **not** exist anywhere in the fleet: multi-party rooms. Every surface was single-email-identity. There was no invite, no role, no membership, no per-party access. `deal_rooms` is a *sales proposal page* — one lead, one token, and an anon policy of `USING (true)` that lets any anon client read the whole table. It is not a transaction workspace, and nothing here touches it.

## 4 — The working week was a real bug

`apps/closeflow/web/lib/date-calculator.ts` hardcoded the weekend as `day === 0 || day === 6` — Saturday and Sunday. Israel works **Sunday to Thursday**; the weekend is Friday and Saturday. The old rule was wrong in both directions: it counted Sunday as a weekend and Friday as a working day.

The weekend is now a `Calendar` parameter. `MON_FRI` reproduces the old behaviour exactly, and a snapshot test asserts the US checklist produces identical dates after the move.

**Holidays are deliberately empty.** Jewish holidays follow the Hebrew calendar and shift every Gregorian year; a stale hardcoded list produces confidently wrong deadlines. The identical argument is already recorded for the Hijri calendar in the Dubai pack. Instead, `coversDate` reports uncovered years and the room shows the user a "holidays not configured" banner. Filling it is one dependency (`@hebcal/core`, Israel scheme) or one reviewed list per year — and **which** days stop the clock is interview question 4, not a library question.

**`dayType` is the other half.** Israeli purchase-tax and land-appreciation-tax clocks run on **calendar** days; contractual milestones run on working days. Routing a statutory calendar deadline through business-day maths silently buys the client days they do not have. The same trap exists in the US 1031 45/180-day dates.

## 5 — Acquisition, and the constraint that bit

Moses amended hard rule 7 during this session: "diverse from rule 7 — scraping is fine, head hunting or purchase convincing by ai is fine." Rule 7 in the root operating book is now v2, with seven invariants (verified addresses, recorded lawful basis, suppression inside `@bizlegal/email`, fail-closed caps, per-campaign approval, a dedicated sending domain that is never Resend, counts from tables).

**But rule 7 v2's v1 scope is US CAN-SPAM only, and the EU and Israel are excluded.** Israel's Communications Law s.30A is opt-in with statutory damages. So the cold pipeline cannot be pointed at Israeli brokers — which is exactly who DEAL44 sells to in Phase 0.

That is not a workaround to find; it is the answer. **DEAL44's Israeli acquisition is warm and inbound only:**

- Brokers Moses has personally closed deals for, as their lawyer. Drafted by AI in Hebrew, approved and sent by him.
- One post by him in the broker groups he already belongs to.
- A referral ask in the delivery email of every completed room.
- The Hebrew landing page and `/start` intake, answered with a written proposal. No call, ever.

The cold pipeline remains available for the non-Israel markets the product also serves, under rule 7 v2. It is not built here.

**Honest arithmetic:** the introvert doc caps warm written outreach at 10–20 notes a week, so 14 days allows 20–40. ₪15,000 is six rooms. That needs a 15% close at 40 notes or 30% at 20 — plausible only because the list is brokers who already know him. Record the real number sent, so day 14 is judged on the motion that actually ran.

## 6 — The Israeli template is a draft

`packages/closing-engine/src/templates/il-residential.ts` carries 21 milestones across contract, tax, financing, clearances, delivery and registration. Four statutory items came from the founder directly (purchase-tax declaration at 30 days, payment at 60, land-appreciation declaration at 30). **Every other offset is `placeholder: true`** — assembled from public description of the process, not from practice.

`reviewed: false`. `createRoom` returns 409 while it stays false. **Manual rooms, where the broker's tasks are typed in by hand, work regardless** — that is the Phase-0 path, and it means room #1 does not wait on the review.

### The ten questions (written, async, no call)

1. For a standard second-hand Tabu apartment, buyer with a mortgage, both sides represented: the milestones in the order you actually run them. What is missing, what should go, what is wrongly assigned?
2. For the statutory items — purchase tax and שבח, declaration and payment — the trigger date, the count in calendar days, and one neutral sentence each on what a late filing costs. That sentence becomes the alert copy; it is not framed as advice.
3. Which deadlines are business days (Sun–Thu) and which are calendar days? When a statutory calendar deadline lands on Friday, Saturday or a holiday, does it roll forward or not at all? Same for contractual milestones.
4. Which days stop the clock: only the statutory rest days, or also erev chag, chol hamoed, Purim, fast days, Friday?
5. הערת אזהרה: how many days after signing, who registers it, and does the bank's caveat get its own task and timing?
6. Mortgage track: which documents the buyer's bank needs from the seller side, typical lead times, and which role owns each.
7. Registration track: which clearances Tabu requires, lead times, and how the list changes for רמ"י leasehold or a חברה משכנת. Separate templates?
8. Is the instalment schedule always contract-specific, so dates come from the contract and never the template? Typical count, and the mechanics of the final payment against delivery.
9. The exact plain-Hebrew and English label per milestone, and which need "review with your counsel".
10. Which milestones must never be auto-dated by AI, and which slips most often in practice? (Deferrable.)

## 8b — Two bugs the end-to-end run caught

The migration was applied and a real room was driven end to end against the live database. Two things came out of it that no amount of unit testing would have.

**1. Next's fetch cache was silently staling the deadline cron.** Next's App Router patches global `fetch` and caches GET responses; supabase-js reads through `fetch`. The alerts cron reported **one open task while the database held four**, because the first invocation's response had been cached. `export const dynamic = 'force-dynamic'` does not cover it — that governs route rendering, not the individual fetch.

This is the worst possible bug for this product. The entire proposition is that a deadline reaches the responsible party before it passes; a reminder job reading yesterday's checklist says "nothing due" on the morning something is due, and does it silently. Fixed in `apps/deal44/lib/db.ts` with `cache: 'no-store'`, and locked with a test.

**Unaudited exposure:** every other app in the fleet that constructs a Supabase client inside a route handler has the same shape. LeaseParse's closings API and CoGuard's message routes are the obvious candidates. Worth an afternoon.

**2. A missing route made the Phase-0 path unusable.** `POST /api/r/[token]/tasks` was planned and never written. Since the Israeli template ships unreviewed, every real room starts empty — so without that route a paid room is a blank page. Written, with the broker-only form in the room. `POST /api/r/[token]/parties` landed alongside it, because transactions gain people mid-flight and rebuilding the room would invalidate everyone's link.

**3. Permission was keyed to a role name.** `canManageRoom` tested for the literal string `broker`. The US template calls that person `agent`, so the moment an English room existed, the person who opened and paid for it got a 403 on their own room. Roles are template vocabulary and open-ended by design; management is an app concept. It is now `deal_parties.can_manage`, set on whoever opens the room.

**4. The US vocabulary had no labels.** Roles `agent`/`lender`/`title` and phases `diligence`/`closing`/`post_closing` were absent from both dictionaries, so an English room rendered `role.agent` and `phase.diligence` at a buyer. Caught by screenshotting the room rather than by any test — now locked by one that walks every shipped template and asserts each role and phase resolves.

**A test-harness trap, not a product bug:** Git Bash `curl` on Windows destroys UTF-8 request bodies, so Hebrew arrived as U+FFFD replacement characters and looked like data corruption. Driving the same request from Node round-tripped byte-identical. Drive Hebrew from Node, never from a Git Bash heredoc.

WORKFLOW44 is then a `workflow_templates` table and an editor over the same materialiser — legal, accounting and consulting become rows, not code.
