### DAY 5 — Compliance Tip (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Compliance Tip — GDPR Data Mapping

---

If you process EU personal data, you need a Record of Processing Activities (RoPA).

Most founders have heard of it. Almost none have one that would survive a DPA audit.

Here's what a defensible RoPA actually requires:

1. **Every processing activity named** — not "marketing" but "email nurture via Klaviyo for leads from Meta Ads"
2. **Legal basis per activity** — consent, legitimate interest, contract, legal obligation. Pick one. Document the reasoning.
3. **Data categories specified** — "name and email" is different from "name, email, IP, and behavioral clickstream"
4. **Retention period per category** — "we keep it forever" is not an answer
5. **Third-party processors listed** — your Stripe, your Hubspot, your AWS region
6. **Transfer mechanism for non-EEA processors** — SCCs, adequacy decision, or binding corporate rules

The DPA won't call ahead. A breach notification triggers a records request within 72 hours.

Build the RoPA now. It takes 4 hours. A fine for missing it starts at €10M or 2% of global turnover.

### DAY 11 — Compliance Tip (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Compliance Tip — BOI / FinCEN

---

If your US LLC or corporation was formed before January 1, 2024, your BOI report was due on January 1, 2025.

If you haven't filed yet, you're in violation of the Corporate Transparency Act.

The penalty: $591 per day. Intentional violations can carry criminal charges.

Here's what Beneficial Ownership Information reporting requires:

**For the company:**
- Full legal name
- All trade names (DBAs)
- Principal US address
- Jurisdiction of formation
- EIN

**For each beneficial owner** (anyone with 25%+ ownership OR substantial control):
- Full legal name
- Date of birth
- Residential address
- A government-issued photo ID (passport or driver's license)

Exemptions exist — but "small company" is not an automatic exemption. You have to meet all three prongs: fewer than 20 FTE employees, under $5M in gross receipts, and a physical US office.

Don't assume you're exempt. Verify.

The FinCEN portal is free to use. The filing takes 20 minutes. The penalty for skipping it is $591/day.

### DAY 13 — Regulatory Hot Take (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Regulatory Hot Take — MiCA Article 16

---

MiCA's reserve requirements for e-money tokens are not what most teams think they are.

Article 36 requires 100% reserve backing. But Article 37 specifies *how* that reserve must be held: at least 30% in credit institutions, with strict limits on investment in money market funds.

Here's where projects are getting tripped up:

**You cannot hold your reserve in stablecoins.** If your EMT is backed by USDC, you have a circular reserve problem that the ECB has flagged explicitly.

**You cannot hold the reserve on-chain by default.** The custody requirements point toward regulated custodians, not a multisig wallet.

**Your whitepaper must describe the reserve composition.** Auditors will check it quarterly.

The teams that are MiCA-ready aren't the ones with the best tech. They're the ones that built the legal entity, appointed the CTO as CISO, hired an MLRO, engaged a statutory auditor, and documented every policy before filing with their NCA.

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Regulatory Hot Take — DAO Legal Wrappers

---

Courts keep treating DAOs as general partnerships.

The most recent example: a DeFi protocol lost a motion to dismiss because the court found that token holders who voted on governance proposals were, functionally, partners in a general partnership — with unlimited joint and several liability.

This is not a fringe interpretation. It's consistent with Ooki DAO, with Sarcuni v. bZx, and with the CFTC's enforcement posture.

The answer isn't to avoid governance. It's to wrap the governance in a legal entity that creates liability separation.

Practical options:

**Wyoming DAO LLC** — explicit statutory recognition, but must name members, defeats pseudonymity

**Marshall Islands DAO** — offshore, explicit recognition, used by several major protocols

**Foundation + Ops Company structure** — foundation owns the IP and governs, ops company executes contracts and employs people; popular in Cayman/Zug setups

**Unincorporated Nonprofit Association** — recognized in some US states, underused, worth exploring for smaller DAOs

None of these are free. All of them are cheaper than being a named defendant in a CFTC action.

If you're running a DAO with real economic activity and you don't have a legal wrapper, that's not a philosophical choice — it's a liability accumulation strategy.

### DAY 18 — Compliance Tip (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Compliance Tip — Vendor Due Diligence

---

Your compliance program is only as strong as your weakest vendor.

Regulators treat your third-party processors, data sub-processors, and payment partners as extensions of your compliance perimeter.

Here's the vendor due diligence checklist that actually holds up under audit:

**Tier 1 (payment processors, core data processors):**
- Copy of their AML/BSA program (not a summary — the actual policy)
- Proof of registration with FinCEN or equivalent
- Last 12 months of SAR filing capability documentation
- DPA or data processing addendum with SCCs if they touch EU data
- SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 cert

**Tier 2 (SaaS tools that touch customer data):**
- DPA with sub-processor list
- Privacy policy that matches your own retention standards
- Contractual right to audit or receive audit reports

**Tier 3 (tools with no customer data):**
- Basic security questionnaire
- Log annually

The mistake I see most: teams assume a big vendor name = compliance. Stripe is GDPR-compliant as a platform. But if you're not using the right DPA addendum and haven't configured data residency, you're still the one on the hook.

Document the vendor stack. Review it quarterly. When a vendor changes their sub-processor list (they will), you need to catch it.

### DAY 22 — Regulatory Hot Take (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Regulatory Hot Take — Travel Rule

---

The FATF Travel Rule is in force in 40+ jurisdictions. Most crypto companies are still not compliant.

Here's the technical problem: the Travel Rule requires VASPs to share originator and beneficiary information with every transfer. But the blockchain doesn't include that data. And there's no universal protocol for attaching it.

The current solutions — TRISA, OpenVASP, Notabene, Sygna — are all interoperability frameworks. None of them have universal adoption. Which means your Travel Rule solution needs to handle:

1. **Counterparty discovery** — is the receiving wallet a VASP? Which one? Are they registered?
2. **Protocol handshake** — can your system talk to theirs?
3. **Data transmission** — name, address, account ID, transaction amount must be transmitted *before* the transaction settles
4. **Fallback for unhosted wallets** — FinCEN proposed requiring collection above $3K; some EU states go further
5. **Record retention** — 5 years in most jurisdictions

If you're running a crypto business and you haven't shipped a Travel Rule solution, you cannot legally transfer assets to/from other VASPs in most regulated jurisdictions.

The deadline was 2022 in the US. The enforcement is coming.

Which Travel Rule provider are you using? Any interoperability nightmares to share?

### DAY 25 — Compliance Tip (LI)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Category:** Compliance Tip — Incident Response Plan

---

Most founders don't have an incident response plan until after the incident.

Here's the minimum viable IRP for a B2B SaaS company handling personal data:

**Detection (0-1 hour):**
- Named person on call with authority to declare an incident
- Monitoring that fires on unusual data access patterns, failed logins, or large data exports
- Pre-built escalation chain: engineer → CTO → CEO → legal

**Containment (1-4 hours):**
- Playbook for isolating affected systems without destroying forensic evidence
- Communication hold until scope is understood — no public statements in hour one
- Preserve logs before anything is patched

**Assessment (4-24 hours):**
- Is personal data affected? (If yes: GDPR clock starts — 72 hours to notify DPA)
- How many data subjects?
- Is there ongoing harm? (Medical data, financial credentials = higher urgency)
- Root cause identified?

**Notification:**
- GDPR: DPA within 72 hours if there's a risk to individuals. Data subjects if high risk.
- CCPA: specific California requirements for certain data categories
- SEC: if you're a public company, material cyber incidents now require 8-K within 4 business days

**Post-Incident:**
- Written lessons-learned within 30 days
- Control improvement documented
- Evidence of remediation for future audit

None of this is optional under GDPR. Write the plan before you need it.

Here's why they're a minefield:

**AML:** FinCEN's Geographic Targeting Orders (GTOs) require title companies and real estate professionals to collect and report beneficial ownership information on cash purchases above thresholds in major metros. But the CDD rule for real estate has been delayed so many times that many practitioners don't know where they stand.

**FBAR + FATCA:** US persons buying property through foreign entities need to report the holding structure. Many don't. The penalties are significant.

**Withholding:** FIRPTA requires buyers to withhold 15% of the purchase price when buying from foreign sellers — even if the seller is a foreign person who owns a US LLC. Most buyers' counsel misses this.

**Beneficial ownership:** Post-CTA, the holding entity for the property needs a BOI filing. If the foreign parent is the beneficial owner, that creates disclosure obligations for a non-US person.

**Transfer pricing:** Intra-family or related-party deals between jurisdictions trigger arm's-length documentation requirements.

None of these are exotic edge cases. They come up in virtually every cross-border deal above $500K.

The compliance layer on real estate transactions is both thick and under-resourced. That's a market.
