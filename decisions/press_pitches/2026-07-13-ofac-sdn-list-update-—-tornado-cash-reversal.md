The Fifth Circuit's November 2024 ruling in Van Loon v. Department of the Treasury (overturning OFAC's SDN listing of Tornado Cash's immutable smart contracts) is one of the most consequential OFAC decisions for crypto compliance programs in the last five years — and it's still being misread.

A few angles I can speak to from active compliance work:

1. **What Van Loon actually held (and didn't).** The Fifth Circuit held that immutable smart contracts are not "property" under IEEPA and therefore cannot be SDN-listed. It did not hold that OFAC cannot list the Tornado Cash Foundation, its operators, or mutable smart contract versions. The compliance consequence for most programs is narrower than the headlines suggest.

2. **How to rebuild your OFAC screening framework after the ruling.** Most crypto compliance programs were calibrated around OFAC's 2022 action. The reversal creates a gap: the screening logic that flagged TC-associated addresses may now produce false positives (or misidentify legitimate transactions) without a policy update. I can walk through what an updated framework looks like.

3. **The next target.** DOJ and OFAC have pivoted to individual targeting post-Van Loon. Roman Storm's criminal trial is proceeding on a theory that doesn't require IEEPA property analysis. Compliance programs should be screening operators and founders, not just contract addresses.
