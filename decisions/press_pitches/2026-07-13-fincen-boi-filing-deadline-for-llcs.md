Hi {journalist_first_name},

The BOI/CTA story has been consistently underreported as a filing story. As a commercial attorney who works with LLCs and holding structures, the more interesting story is the liability architecture — specifically, who is exposed and under what theory.

A few angles worth covering:

1. **The certifying officer exposure.** BOI requires a "certifying individual" to attest accuracy. Most LLCs filed their initial BOI using the organizer or registered agent. If that person is no longer associated with the company and the report is now stale, the liability for the false certification sits on whoever's name appears. This creates a quiet time-bomb in single-member LLCs where ownership has transferred without a BOI update.

2. **The court injunction / reinstatement cycle.** FinCEN's enforcement was enjoined in late 2024 (NSBA v. Yellen), then reinstated, then partially re-enjoined. The resulting confusion means a meaningful percentage of companies that relied on counsel to monitor the situation have no clear filing status. I can explain the current posture accurately.

3. **DAO and tokenized LLC exposure.** Decentralized governance structures with real-world LLC wrappers are almost certainly "reporting companies" under the CTA — and few have filed. This is the compliance gap nobody's writing about.
