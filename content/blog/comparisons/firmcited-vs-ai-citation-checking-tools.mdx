# AI Citation Checking for Law Firms: FirmCited vs Churci, Ascero, and KeyCite (2026)

AI-generated legal writing has created a new malpractice risk: fabricated citations. Courts have sanctioned attorneys for filing briefs with cases that do not exist — the *Mata v. Avianca* matter is the most widely reported example. This article compares the tools that check citations, honestly, so a firm can pick the right layer of protection.

**Last updated:** 2026-09-06 · 8 min read

## Quick Answer

Citation-checking tools fall into four tiers: **per-citation checkers** (Churci, ~$10-$30 per batch), **subscription verifiers** (Ascero AI, $79/month or $29/document), **traditional citators** (KeyCite, Shepard's, BCite — included with Westlaw/Lexis/Bloomberg), and **full-brief audits** (FirmCited, $490 per audit). Per-citation tools catch fabricated cases cheaply. Citators tell you whether a real case is still good law. A full-brief audit does both across an entire filing — which is what a partner should want before a brief goes out the door.

## The problem: two failure modes

AI citation errors come in two distinct flavors, and most tools only catch one:

1. **Fabricated authority.** The AI cites a case that does not exist, or a real case with a wrong name, wrong reporter, or wrong holding. This is the failure mode that gets lawyers sanctioned.
2. **Misapplied authority.** The case is real, but it does not actually support the proposition it is cited for — or it has been overruled, reversed, or limited since the brief was drafted.

A complete citation check needs to catch both. Traditional citators (KeyCite, Shepard's, BCite) are excellent at the second — they track the subsequent history of real cases. They cannot catch the first, because a fabricated case has no history to track. That gap is exactly what the new AI citation tools are built to close.

## When each makes sense

**Per-citation checkers (Churci and similar).** The cheapest layer. If you have a handful of citations to verify — a memo, a short motion — a $10-$30 batch check is the right call. These tools are built for the solo practitioner and small firm.

**Subscription verifiers (Ascero and similar).** For firms that file briefs every week, a flat monthly or per-document price makes sense. You get extraction of every citation in a draft plus verification, at a price that scales with volume.

**Traditional citators (KeyCite, Shepard's, BCite).** You already pay for these inside Westlaw, Lexis, or Bloomberg. They are the right tool for the "is this case still good law" question — and they are the wrong tool for the "does this case exist" question. Use them, but do not assume they cover the AI-hallucination risk.

**Full-brief audits (FirmCited).** When a brief is about to be filed, the question is not "is this one citation good" but "is every citation in this filing good." A $490 audit covers the whole brief — existence, support, and history — and produces an evidentiary-grade report you can keep in the matter file. For a partner signing a brief, that is cheap insurance against a sanctions motion.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a citator and an AI citation checker?

**A citator (KeyCite, Shepard's, BCite) tracks the subsequent history of real cases** — whether a case was affirmed, reversed, overruled, or limited. **An AI citation checker verifies that a citation exists and supports the proposition it is cited for.** The two catch different errors. A fabricated case has no history, so a citator cannot flag it; that is the gap the AI tools fill.

### How much does citation checking cost?

**From $10 per batch to $490 per full-brief audit.** Per-citation tools run roughly $10-$30 per batch. Subscription verifiers run about $79/month or $29/document. Citators are included in Westlaw/Lexis/Bloomberg subscriptions. Full-brief audits run a few hundred dollars.

### Can a citation checker guarantee my brief has no bad citations?

**No tool can guarantee that.** Citation checkers reduce the risk substantially, but verification depends on the underlying databases, and the law changes. The professional standard remains: the attorney signing the filing is responsible for its accuracy. A citation audit is evidence of care, not a substitute for it.

### Do I still need KeyCite or Shepard's if I use an AI citation checker?

**Yes.** The AI tools verify existence and support; the citators verify continued validity. A real case that was overruled last month is a citation problem that only the citator will catch. The strongest workflow uses both.

### What is the *Mata v. Avianca* case?

In 2023, a federal court sanctioned two lawyers and their firm for submitting a brief containing six fabricated judicial opinions and citations generated by ChatGPT. The case became the standard cautionary tale for AI-assisted legal writing and is widely cited in ethics opinions on AI use in litigation.
