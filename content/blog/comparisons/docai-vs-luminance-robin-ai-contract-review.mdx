# DocAI vs Luminance vs Robin AI: AI Contract Review Compared (2026)

AI contract review tools are not one market. There are three very different products wearing the same label: enterprise contract lifecycle platforms, per-scan review tools, and lawyers. This article compares them honestly so you can pick the one that fits your actual situation.

**Last updated:** 2026-09-06 · 8 min read

## Quick Answer

If you need to review **one contract** — a vendor MSA, a customer agreement, a partnership deal — the realistic options are a flat-fee scan like **DocAI ($97 per scan)**, an enterprise platform like **Luminance, Robin AI, or LawGeex (quote-based, annual contracts)**, or a **lawyer ($300-$800/hour)**. Enterprise platforms are built for legal departments managing thousands of contracts; they are overkill for a single review. DocAI is built for the founder or small team that needs a practitioner-grade risk read on one contract, fast, without an annual commitment.

## The landscape in 2026

The AI contract review category splits into three tiers:

**Enterprise contract review platforms.** Luminance, Robin AI, and LawGeex are the names you will see in most "best AI contract review" roundups. All three target enterprise legal departments, all three use **quote-based pricing** (no public tiers — you contact sales), and all three assume you have a contract volume that justifies a platform deployment.

- **Luminance** positions around end-to-end contract lifecycle management with deep integrations (Word, SharePoint, Salesforce, DocuSign, SAP) and a multi-model AI approach.
- **Robin AI** is built on Anthropic's Claude and emphasizes chat-based contract conversations and semantic search.
- **LawGeex** focuses on pre-signature review of routine agreements (NDAs, MSAs, vendor contracts) against your company's encoded legal playbook — but it is enterprise-only and requires upfront playbook setup.

**Per-scan review tools.** This is where DocAI sits. You upload a contract, the system runs a structured risk review against a practitioner checklist, and you get a report. No deployment, no annual contract, no sales call. DocAI charges **$97 per scan** with a free preview.

**Lawyers.** The traditional option. A commercial lawyer reviewing a mid-size agreement commonly bills $300-$800/hour, and a thorough review of a vendor MSA can run 2-6 hours. You get judgment, negotiation support, and jurisdiction-specific advice — at a price and a turnaround that do not fit every situation.

## When each makes sense

**Choose DocAI if:** you have one or a handful of contracts to review, you want a structured risk read this week, and you do not want to talk to a sales team or sign an annual contract. The $97 flat price means the decision is trivial compared to a quote-based platform.

**Choose an enterprise platform if:** you are a legal department managing thousands of contracts, you need contract lifecycle management (storage, workflow, e-signature), and you have the budget and the deployment appetite. These tools are genuinely powerful — they are just not built for the single-contract case.

**Choose a lawyer if:** the contract is high-stakes (acquisition, financing, joint venture), multi-jurisdictional, or already in dispute. No tool replaces legal judgment in those situations. The honest answer is that a lawyer is the right call for the top 10-20% of contracts — and overkill for the rest.

## FAQ

### Is DocAI a replacement for Luminance or Robin AI?

**No.** They solve different problems. Luminance and Robin AI are enterprise contract lifecycle platforms for legal departments with high contract volume. DocAI is a flat-fee per-scan risk review for founders and small teams reviewing one contract at a time. If you manage thousands of contracts, you need a platform. If you have one MSA to review, a platform is overkill.

### How much does Luminance or Robin AI cost?

**Neither publishes pricing.** Both use quote-based, annual contracts sized to contract volume and seat count. Reports from legal ops teams commonly describe five-figure annual commitments for mid-size deployments, but the only way to get a real number is to contact their sales teams.

### How much does a lawyer charge to review a contract?

**$300-$800/hour is a common range**, and a thorough review of a mid-size vendor or customer agreement can run 2-6 hours. For a simple NDA, many firms offer flat fees. The cost is justified for high-stakes or complex contracts — less so for routine ones.

### What does DocAI actually check?

The scan runs a practitioner checklist: liability and indemnification exposure, termination and auto-renewal terms, data processing and privacy obligations, IP ownership, confidentiality scope, payment terms, and change-of-control provisions. The output is a structured risk report, not a legal opinion.

### When should I still hire a lawyer after an AI scan?

**When the contract is high-stakes, multi-jurisdictional, or already in dispute.** An AI scan is a risk screen, not legal advice. For an acquisition, a financing, or any contract where the downside of a missed clause is existential, a lawyer's review is the right investment.
